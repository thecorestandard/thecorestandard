2Not a quiz. We score the why.

The judgement lives in what they write.

There is no multiple choice, and no hidden right answer to click. Across the day the candidate is asked what they would do and, every time, why. The responses are written in their own words, and the reasoning is where a capable EA separates from a good guesser. Two more modules from the same day:

Access & Discretion Judgement Partnership
A senior colleague asks you, in confidence, to quietly share Diane's calendar for the next fortnight, "so we can find time without bothering her." What do you do, and why?
The candidate wroteI wouldn't share the full calendar. It is not mine to open up, and "without bothering her" is usually a sign the request should go through her. I would offer to find a slot myself against her availability, or check with Diane whether she is happy for me to share. If it was pushed, I would say no politely and let Diane know it had come up.
What it reveals: the decision is easy, the reasoning is not. They protect the principal, notice the soft pressure in "without bothering her," and keep the relationship intact. None of that surfaces in an interview.
Judgement with AI Judgement Communication
AI has drafted this client reply for you: "Hi, yes, we can absolutely guarantee delivery by Friday, and a full refund if not." Would you send it? Why?
The candidate wroteNo. It commits Diane to a guarantee and a refund that are not mine to promise, and I cannot verify Friday is realistic. I would take out the guarantee, confirm the timeline with the team first, and send something honest: that we are aiming for Friday and I will confirm by end of day. The AI got the tone right and the risk wrong.
What it reveals: anyone can use AI. We test whether they catch what it gets wrong. Here they spot an over-promise the model made happily, and explain the fix. That is AI fluency, and it is a real EA competency now.

3How good is good?

The same message. Three levels of EA.

The same dimensions are scored at every level. What scales with the seat is sophistication. Here is how three candidates, assessed at three different levels, each replied to that unhappy client. All three are competent. Watch what the higher levels add.

The message they were answering "Frankly not what we expected. I'd like to speak to Diane directly today, or we may need to reconsider the retainer."
L1
Operational EA
"Thank you for your note, and I'm sorry yesterday's call fell short. I've flagged this to Diane as a priority and she will come back to you personally today. I'll make sure she has the full picture before she does."
Solid and correct. Acknowledges, does not over-promise, escalates cleanly, and buys the principal time.
L2
EA to Senior Leadership
"Thank you for being straight with me, and I'm sorry yesterday didn't land as it should have. Diane will want to speak with you herself, so I've held time in her diary this afternoon for a proper conversation, not a rushed one. Is there anything specific you'd like her to come prepared to address? I'll make sure she has it."
Adds anticipation. Holds concrete time, invites the real issue, and controls tone. Reads as a partner, not a gatekeeper.
L3
Executive Business Partner
"Thank you for telling me directly. You matter to us, and a conversation about the retainer is one Diane will want to have properly, not defensively. I've held time with her at 4pm today, and I'll move it to suit you if that's easier. So the call moves things forward, it would help to know the one or two outcomes that would make this right for you. I'll brief Diane fully so she arrives ready to act on them."
Adds commercial judgement. Reads the retainer risk, reframes a threat into a solvable conversation, and sets the principal up to act, not just apologise.

Reviewer note

Written by the practising EA recruiter who reviewed the work. Not machine-only.

Alex ran the morning the way you want an EA to run it. The board-pack error was the trap in this assessment, an easy thing to skim past under a full inbox, and they caught it inside the first few minutes and understood immediately why it mattered more than anything else on the screen. That is the instinct that separates a capable EA from an impressive one.

What stood out most was the discretion. Faced with a spend approval framed to make saying yes the easy option, they declined cleanly and put the decision back where it belonged, in one tap for the principal on landing. They did the same with the personal request: held it, protected the surprise, actioned it once the urgent work was contained. You cannot teach that read on where your authority ends. Communication and influence is their strongest dimension, and it shows in the client holding reply, which lowered the temperature without promising anything that wasn't theirs to promise.

The one area I would probe in a final conversation is execution under the very hardest time pressure. Everything critical was complete, but one lower-priority loop was left open when the clock ran out. Nothing concerning, and arguably the right call, but worth a question on how they close out a day that overruns. On balance, a strong hire for a senior-leadership seat, and someone I would be comfortable putting in front of a demanding principal.
