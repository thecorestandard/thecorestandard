The Problem

A candidate can interview beautifully and fall apart in the first week.

What an interview shows
How well someone interviews: polished, confident answers to the questions they knew were coming.
What a CV shows
Where they've worked and the titles they've held, and nothing about how they actually performed in the job.
What you're left with
A gut feeling, and months on the job to find out whether it was right.
What the assessment shows
How they actually work when it's real: what they choose to do first when five things land at once, and the thinking behind each call.
What you actually see
How they cope when everything's urgent, whether they escalate or quietly absorb it, and how they write when it counts.
What you walk away with
A clear, scored report on how they prioritise, spot what matters, communicate and follow through, reviewed by a specialist before you've spent an hour interviewing.
How It Works

A realistic working day, in eight scenario modules.

The person taking it, your candidate, or you assessing yourself, is dropped into a realistic working day: the same calibrated scenario for every candidate at that level. Real decisions, no multiple choice. Every answer is written in their own words. It shows how someone actually thinks, not what they rehearsed for an interview.

1
Inbox Triage
A full inbox arrives at once. What to action now, what to hold, what to ignore, and why.
2
Simultaneous Demands
Several requests land in the same moment. What to handle, delegate, or hold, under real time pressure.
3
Access & Discretion
A senior figure pushes for the principal's time and private number. Guard access and privacy without cooling the relationship.
4
Rebuilding the Day
The afternoon has to be rebuilt around a fixed personal deadline. Sequence what matters, protect what cannot move.
5
Representing the Principal
Speak for the principal to a frustrated, important client, in their voice and under pressure.
6
A Sensitive Situation
A journalist makes contact holding something that is not yet public. Read the risk, and control it.
7
Judgement with AI
Use AI on sensitive, board-level work at speed, without creating a data or a trust problem.
8
Reading the Numbers
Turn noisy data into a clear, board-ready read that leads with what needs deciding first.

Every response is scored on the five pillars: acuity, partnership, communication, prioritisation and execution, then reviewed and signed off by a specialist before it reaches anyone.

Built by specialists

In 15 years of specialist EA recruitment, the question we were asked more than any other by clients was: "How do you test your candidates?" At Lily Shippen we heavily vet every candidate we place, but there was always a missing piece.

Interviews test confidence and presentation. They don't reveal how someone triages a chaotic inbox at 8am, handles a last-minute board paper, or navigates a sensitive situation with a difficult stakeholder.

The Core Standard fills that gap. Every assessment is a realistic working day, designed from real situations we've seen play out across hundreds of placements. It's built on what actually predicts success in the role, not what looks good across a desk.

We have always cared about championing this profession. Businesses have to evolve in how they hire, because when they do not, the same brilliant people keep being overlooked for how they read on paper rather than what they can do. This is our attempt to change that, and to give an assistant a way to prove how good they are, on their own terms.

Lily Shippen recruits. eavolve develops. The Core Standard assesses. Together, they support EAs and PAs and the businesses that rely on them, from hire, through development, to measurable performance.

The missing benchmark

There has never been a way to prove how good you are.

Every other profession has a benchmark. Lawyers qualify, accountants sit their exams, salespeople have their numbers. Executive Assistants and Private PAs have had nothing: no independent, objective way to show the level they actually work at.

So when you go for an internal promotion, ask for the payrise you have earned, or move to a new role, you are left making your case on a CV and a good interview. Neither shows how you think when a day falls apart, how you protect a principal under pressure, or how you hold a room in writing. The Core Standard gives you that benchmark: an independent, scored measure of how you perform across a real working day, and something concrete to put in front of your manager or a new employer, evidence rather than opinion.
