Why assess an executive assistant before you hire

A candidate can interview with poise and fall apart in the first week.

CVs tell you where someone has been. Interviews tell you how they present. Neither tells you how they’ll actually handle your priorities, your stakeholders, or the pressure of the role.

The Core Standard puts candidates inside a realistic working day and scores them across five dimensions that actually predict success.

  1. Prioritisation. What gets done first when everything is urgent, and whether the principal’s agenda stays front of mind.
  2. Judgement. Catching the clash in the diary, the risk buried in an email, and the thing the principal has not said yet.
  3. Communication. Writing in the principal’s voice: concise, warm, and right for the audience.
  4. Execution. Turning a half-sentence brief into a finished result, and closing the loop so nothing slips through.
  5. Partnership. Working as a genuine partner: anticipating needs, holding relationships on the principal’s behalf, and earning the trust to act in their name.

Every pillar is scored 0 to 100 against behavioural anchors, and the senior levels add AI Fluency, Judgement & Ethics and Data Interpretation. Read how each pillar is scored in the methodology →

Inside the simulation

What the executive assistant assessment actually tests.

No multiple choice, no typing tests. The candidate is dropped into a realistic EA working day at a venture-backed software business, nine modules in the order a real day throws them, and has to decide, write and prioritise under a running clock. You see how they think, not what they have rehearsed. See the full day, module by module →

1

Inbox triage

A full inbox and twelve minutes. What goes to the principal now, what waits, what gets ignored, and why.

2

A diary that falls apart

Three things change before the principal arrives. Rebuild the morning without dropping what matters.

3

Writing in the principal’s voice

A reply to a frustrated client, in their name. Tone, clarity and judgement under pressure.

4

Judgement with AI and data

An AI draft with a flaw buried in it, and a set of numbers with a decision inside. Do they catch it?

What employers ask before they commission.

A structured way to see how a candidate does the job before you hire them. The Core Standard is a scenario-based one: the candidate works through a realistic 97-minute EA working day covering inbox triage, a diary that falls apart, writing in the principal’s voice, a stakeholder dilemma and judgement with AI and data. A specialist reviewer scores it against five pillars, Prioritisation, Judgement, Communication, Execution and Partnership, on a published rubric. It is not a typing test, a multiple-choice quiz or a personality questionnaire.
Interviews show how someone presents. Psychometrics describe self-reported tendencies. Neither captures what a person would do with your inbox at 9am. The Core Standard captures behaviour, in real scenarios, under a running clock. Most clients use it between first and final interview rather than instead of either. First Look, a 30-minute three-module version, is £195 +VAT and is deducted from EA Hire on the same candidate within 30 days.
