About the Standard

The Core Standard is a scenario-based assessment for Executive Assistants, Private PAs, and Chief of Staff hires. Candidates complete a realistic working day across multiple modules, and their approach is scored against a expert-built rubric.

The benchmark, not the average. We do not test answers. We test approach.

Psychometrics measure traits, preferences, and styles. They are predictive of personality, not performance. The Core Standard measures what a candidate actually does when given a realistic workload: what they prioritise, how they write, how they recover from a curveball, and how they close the day.

If you want a personality overlay, add TCS Match. It is an optional extra, never the whole picture.

Interviews measure how someone talks about their work. The Core Standard measures how they do it. Candidates make live decisions under realistic pressure, so employers see judgment, prioritisation, and written communication as they happen rather than as they are recalled.

Lily Shippen and Emily Partridge. Thirteen years of EA and Private PA recruitment, thirteen years sitting at the desk as Chief of Staff and Head of EA at high-growth firms. Every scenario, rubric, and reviewer note has been written by a expert.

The candidate is given an AI-drafted output with a deliberate flaw and asked to intervene, or asked to describe how they would use AI for a specific task and where they would stop trusting it. No, and we tested it directly. We ran the assessment with submissions that leaned heavily on AI, and they did not come out on top. We don’t ban AI in the assessment, because the job doesn’t ban it: a strong EA in 2026 uses it. What AI cannot supply is the judgement the role actually runs on.

The scenarios are built around the decisions AI can’t make for someone: which of three competing demands the principal really cares about today, how to word a difficult message to a board member, when to push back, when to check before acting, and what to protect the principal from. AI will hand you a competent draft; it won’t tell you it’s the wrong thing to send. The highest scores go to candidates who use AI as a capable assistant and then apply their own emotional intelligence, judgement, and EA experience on top, which is exactly what the seat demands. AI plus a great EA beats AI alone, and it beats a great EA who won’t touch AI. The assessment is built to reward the same thing the job does.

Module 8, introduced at Level 3 only. The candidate is given a KPI dashboard or a messy spreadsheet and asked to surface the three things the principal needs to know before the room. One sentence each, ordered by commercial weight.

This is not a spreadsheet skills test. No formulas, no numeracy beyond pattern recognition. The module scores what the candidate chooses to flag, and whether they can tell a genuine signal apart from a false positive. It is the single sharpest predictor we have found of whether an Executive EA will grow into an EBP or plateau at logistics.

Modules 1 to 6 are the immovable skills of the role: Inbox Triage, Simultaneous Demands, Conflicting Priorities, Calendar Rebuild, Email Drafting, Stakeholder Dilemma. They test the working-day spine every EA hires on.

Module 7 (AI Fluency, at L2 and L3) and Module 8 (Data Interpretation, at L3) exist because the profession has moved. They reflect the capability differential we see between competent EAs and outstanding ones in 2026 hiring rounds. Nothing is there to pad the time. The module stack has been rebuilt twice since 2024 and will continue to evolve on a published schedule.

Scoring & fairness

Every response is scored against a published, behaviourally-anchored rubric across five pillars, Prioritisation, Judgement, Communication, Execution, and Partnership, and each pillar is rated on a 0 to 100 benchmark. AI generates the first pass. An expert reviews, adjusts, and signs off every report.

Red flags cap the relevant pillar at 1 for that module. Standout moves are logged and carry through to the reviewer narrative.

Lily Shippen reviews and signs off every report, across every stream, with thirteen years of specialist EA recruitment directly on the page. The rubric is co-authored with Emily Partridge, a former EA Team Manager, and the reviewer pool recalibrates against it quarterly. Every report carries a reviewer sign-off block.

Chief of Staff

No. Chief of Staff is a separate stream, not a higher EA level. It tests proxy decision-making, influence without authority, board-level written communication, and strategic synthesis. The scenarios, rubric, and reviewer are distinct.

A multi-stakeholder scenario built around a fictional group CEO. Candidates handle a board prep pack, arbitrate between two warring function heads, draft a strategic memo, respond to a live escalation, and close with a weekly priorities brief to the CEO.

Duration: 90 minutes. Report length: sixteen to twenty pages.
