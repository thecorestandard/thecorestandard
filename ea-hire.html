What the test measures

Five pillars. One day of work.

A realistic working day, scored against the dimensions that actually predict success in the seat, not how a candidate performs in an interview room. Every pillar is scored against a published, behaviourally anchored rubric. Read the full methodology →

The Core Standard The framework What we
measure

Not a quiz. Not a personality test. We score how a candidate behaves across a realistic working day. The five below are the core; higher levels add dimensions suited to the seat.

Behaviour,
not credentials.
  1. 01
    Under pressure

    Prioritisation

    Does the candidate recognise what matters most when everything is urgent, and hold the principal's agenda in mind when sequencing the day?

  2. 02
    Signal & nuance

    Judgement

    Do they spot the risk buried in the meeting request, the conflict in the diary, the tone shift in the email? The unflashy work of noticing.

  3. 03
    Tone & audience

    Communication

    Can they write with the voice of the principal? Concise, warm, appropriate to the audience. Poor writing costs relationships.

  4. 04
    Plan to delivery

    Execution

    Do they close the loop? Confirm receipt, flag blockers, and leave the day tidier than they found it. The end-of-day discipline.

  5. 05
    Trust & proxy

    Partnership

    Do they work as a genuine partner, not just a pair of hands? Anticipating what the principal needs before it is asked, holding relationships and stakeholders on their behalf, and earning the trust to act in their name.

The five pillars are scored for every candidate. A candidate who performs at the senior levels is also scored on the dimensions those seats demand.

One assessment

Every candidate sits the same day.

There is one Core Standard assessment, the same one EAs sit for TCS Certified. Everyone on your shortlist works through an identical day, so the results compare like for like. The reviewer places each candidate at Level 1, 2 or 3 from what they did, so you can see at a glance who fits the seat.

The day

A working day at a venture-backed software business: nine modules and four short mindset questions. The morning inbox, two people who need you at once, a conflicting instruction, a diary that falls apart, a reply in the principal’s voice, a stakeholder dilemma, an AI draft with a flaw in it, numbers with a decision buried in them, and a judgement call as the day ends.

The clock

97 minutes, hard stop. The clock starts at the first module, not while the candidate reads the briefing, and the session submits itself at zero. Answers save as they go. The link is single-use, and the candidate has 14 days to sit it on a laptop or desktop, in their own time.

The report

Within 48 hours: the level the candidate demonstrated, a hire recommendation, a score on every pillar, the modules one by one with the reviewer’s read, and the questions to ask at the next stage. Signed by the reviewer. The candidate keeps their own copy and a certificate at the level they reached.

What the level means

The level is the result, not a choice.

You do not pick a level when you commission. The reviewer places each candidate at the level their work demonstrated, so you can see at a glance whether they fit the seat you are filling.

Level 1

Operational EA

Reliable single-principal support. Inbox, diary, travel and internal comms handled cleanly and on time.

Level 2

EA to Senior Leadership

A true right hand to a partner, founder or C-suite principal. Anticipates, prioritises and holds stakeholders under pressure.

Level 3

Executive Business Partner

Second brain to the principal. Strategic oversight, board preparation, sound judgement with data and with AI.

Why it pays for itself

A wrong EA hire costs far more than the salary.

Add the recruitment fee to find them, the onboarding and training, the principal’s time spent re-recruiting, the disruption to the day, and the momentum lost while the seat sits wrong, then the cost of running the whole search again. An assessment is £495: a small fraction of that, and it turns a high-stakes judgement call into an evidenced one, on the shortlist you have already narrowed by interview.
