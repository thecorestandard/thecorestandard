How to use your Recruiter Summary: a recruiter’s view.

One page, a band per pillar, three standout moves and a reviewer’s verdict. It is the most useful document most EAs have never had. Here is where it belongs, where it does not, and how to talk about it.

By Lily Shippen Published 14 September 2026 6 min read

In short: your Recruiter Summary is a one-page, recruiter-ready version of your TCS Certified result. Attach it to applications alongside your CV, send it to a recruiter when you register, lead your interview answers with a standout move, and use it as third-party evidence in a pay or promotion conversation. Do not edit it, do not send the full report unless asked, and do not hide a level that was lower than you hoped. Position it.

For thirteen years I read EA CVs for a living. A good one takes a recruiter thirty seconds, and in those thirty seconds every line is a claim: managed complex diaries, trusted with confidential matters, calm under pressure. I believed some of them. I had no way to check any of them until the candidate was in front of a client, by which point my name was on the introduction. The Recruiter Summary exists because of that thirty seconds. It is the one page I always wished a candidate could hand me.

What it is

When you sit TCS Certified you receive four things: a feedback report of twelve to sixteen pages, a signed certificate with a verifier link, a digital badge, and the Recruiter Summary. The Summary is a one-page Word document written in recruiter language. It carries your name, your level, whether Operational EA, EA to Senior Leadership or Executive Business Partner, a band for each pillar, three standout moves the reviewer saw you make, and the reviewer’s top-line verdict. It does not carry your development pointers or the reviewer’s full commentary. Those are in the report, which is yours and which you decide whether to share.

The point of the split is that the report is for you and the Summary is for them. A recruiter does not need sixteen pages. She needs to know, in the time it takes to read a CV, that a named reviewer watched you work a realistic day and what they saw.

Five places it belongs

With your application, next to your CV

Not instead of it. The CV tells the story, the Summary proves a chapter of it. Attach it as a second document and add one line to your cover note: I have attached my Core Standard Recruiter Summary, an expert-reviewed assessment of how I actually work. Nothing more. Let the page do it.

With a recruiter, the day you register

This is the one I feel most strongly about. When a candidate registers with an agency, the recruiter is deciding in that first conversation which clients to put him in front of. A Summary changes that decision. It gives the recruiter something specific to say to a client that is not “he interviewed well”. Send it with your CV before the call, and mention it once. A good recruiter will ask about the standout moves.

In the interview

“Tell me about yourself” is the weakest ten minutes of most interviews. Your three standout moves are three prepared, evidenced answers. A reviewer watching me work a full day flagged that I re-sequenced around a board-level arrival without prompting, and here is the real version of that from my current role. You have moved from claim to evidence in one sentence, and you have given the interviewer something concrete to probe.

In a pay or promotion conversation

Asking for more is hard for EAs partly because the evidence is usually your own account of your work. The Summary is a third party’s account, against a published standard, with a level attached. I have been assessed at EA to Senior Leadership and I am paid as an Operational EA is a conversation with a shape. Bring the Summary, not the report.

On LinkedIn, as the badge and one line

The badge links to your verifier page and shows the level at a glance. Add one line to your headline or About section: TCS Certified, EA to Senior Leadership. Do not post the Summary itself. It is a document for a conversation, not a feed.

Three places it does not belong

Edited. It is a Word document so that you can attach it anywhere, not so that you can change it. Your certificate carries the same level and a verifier link, and any recruiter or employer can check it. An altered Summary is the fastest way to lose a job you would otherwise have got.

Sent as the full report, unprompted. If an employer asks for the report, share it. That is a good sign. Do not lead with it. Sixteen pages to a hiring manager who has not asked is a page too many, and the development pointers are yours.

Hidden because the level was lower than you hoped. Some people sit Certified expecting Executive Business Partner and demonstrate EA to Senior Leadership. That is not a failure. It is an expert telling you where you sit today against a standard employers hire against, and exactly what would take you to the next level. A Summary at a lower level than your job title, with a Strong or Distinctive band somewhere on it, is still more evidence than the candidate next to you has.

How to talk about the bands

The five bands are Developing, Proficient, Strong, Distinctive and Exemplary, and each is anchored to behaviour, not a feeling. Proficient is a solid, professional level of the pillar. Strong is above the line. Distinctive is the reviewer seeing something they do not see often at that level. If your Summary is mostly Proficient with one Strong, lead with the Strong and be straightforward about the rest: Communication was my standout, and the reviewer noted Prioritisation as the pillar with the most room, which matches what I would say about myself. Interviewers trust candidates who can describe their own shape.

What a recruiter does with it

I will tell you what I did the first time a candidate sent me one. I read the three standout moves, I read the verdict, and I rang a client I had been trying to get in front of for a month. The Summary gave me a reason to call that was not about me. That is the whole thing. It gives the person representing you something true to say.

Common questions

Can I edit my Recruiter Summary?
You can open it, because it is a Word document, but you should not change it. Your certificate carries the same level and a verifier link that any employer can check, and a Summary that does not match the certificate ends the conversation.

Should I put my Certified level on my CV?
Yes, as one line in your qualifications or headline: TCS Certified, EA to Senior Leadership, with the year. Attach the Summary to the application rather than pasting its contents into the CV.

How long is it valid for?
The certificate carries the date you sat and does not expire. The market moves, though, and so do you. Many of the senior EAs I know re-sit every eighteen months or so as a form of calibration, and a recent date reads better than an old one.

What if I disagree with my result?
You can request a review within 14 days of receiving your report. A second reviewer re-scores independently, and if the second review differs by more than one band on any pillar, the report and Summary are rewritten and reissued at no cost.
