A separate stream

Chief of Staff is not Level 4.

A Chief of Staff extends the principal's operating rhythm rather than administering it. The role is proxy: speaking on the principal's behalf, holding functions accountable to the plan, synthesising board-bound material, and closing decisions in rooms the principal is not in.

That capability does not rise out of an Executive Assistant role on seniority alone. It requires comfort operating on evidence not yet fully formed, the written authority to land a point with the board, and the judgment to know when to escalate versus when to decide. Our Chief of Staff scenario, rubric, and reviewer are all purpose-built around these signals.

If you are unsure whether you are hiring a Chief of Staff or an Executive Business Partner, speak to the team. The answer changes the assessment, the reviewer, and the shortlist.

What the assessment measures

Four capabilities. One strategic day.

A single strategic day, scored against the capabilities that decide whether a Chief of Staff can actually hold the seat, not how they present in a conversation.

The Core Standard The framework What we
measure

We score how a candidate operates across a realistic strategic day. These are the capabilities that decide whether they can carry the mandate of the seat.

Behaviour,
not credentials.
  1. 01
    Mandate

    Proxy decision-making

    Does the candidate know where their mandate begins and ends? Can they decide with conviction when the principal is unavailable, and defer cleanly when the call is above their grade?

  2. 02
    Credibility

    Influence without authority

    Can they bring a hostile function head to resolution without leaning on the principal's name? The best Chiefs of Staff carry weight by credibility, not by title.

  3. 03
    Board-ready

    Board-level communication

    Can they write a memo that a board will read carefully? Tight, defensible, appropriately caveated. The assessment produces three short pieces of board-bound writing.

  4. 04
    Synthesis

    Strategic synthesis

    Can they take conflicting inputs from function heads, pressure from the board, and a live operational escalation, and distil it into a recommendation the principal can act on?

Sample scenario preview

What the simulation feels like.

The illustration below is an example designed to show the shape of the assessment. It is not the live scenario candidates actually sit. Every commissioned assessment uses a separate composite scenario held only by candidates and reviewers.

Imagine a Chief of Staff stepping into a growth-stage business on a Monday morning. A board meeting is on the calendar for Friday. Two function heads are circling a stalled budget negotiation. Legal has just flagged a regulatory question that needs a position by end of week. The CEO has asked for a strategic memo but has not yet briefed it.

The candidate has a limited window to triage what matters, sequence the week, and leave a written trail the principal can stand behind. Every input the candidate writes is scored against the rubric and expert-reviewed before the report is released.

Illustrative module shape

    Board prep synthesis

    Turn multiple function-head inputs into a clean board-pack section under time pressure.

  2. 02
    Stakeholder arbitration

    Mediate between two leaders whose positions have hardened into conflict.

  3. 03
    Strategic memo

    Draft a short memo to the principal recommending a position on a contested decision.

  4. 04
    Live escalation

    Respond to an urgent enquiry from a regulatory or legal counterpart: decide, delegate, or escalate.

  5. 05
    Priorities brief

    A short close-out naming the two or three calls the principal must make this week.
