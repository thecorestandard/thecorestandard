How It Works

A realistic working day. Measured. Not an interview.

The Core Standard is the scenario-based executive assistant test: an immersive simulation of a real EA working day, running 60 to 90 minutes depending on the level. Unlike typing-speed tests or multiple-choice executive assistant testing tools, it measures judgement, discretion, priority logic, and communication, not what a candidate has memorised from an interview prep guide.

Inside the Simulation

What the candidate actually does.

The simulation drops the candidate into a role at a fictional organisation. The scenario is written by a specialist EA recruiter and grounded in the real friction of real EA days, not in abstract puzzles.

Over the course of the simulation, the candidate works through a sequence of scenario modules. Six at entry level, eight at senior level. Together they compress a full working day into the session window:

  • Inbox triage. A full inbox lands at once, with roughly fifteen minutes of work compressed into ten or twelve. The candidate decides what to sequence, what to hold, and explains the reasoning behind each call. This module is separately timed.
  • Simultaneous demands. Several competing asks arrive in the same window. The candidate has to decide what moves, what holds, and what gets delegated.
  • Conflicting priorities. An instruction that runs into a complication. We test the reasoning the candidate uses, not the specific answer they land on.
  • Calendar rebuild. The principal's day is no longer viable. The candidate restructures it, protecting what matters.
  • Live drafting. A reply that has to be written in full, not selected from a list. Tone, judgement, and brevity are all visible in the output.
  • Stakeholder dilemma. A conflict with no neat answer. How the candidate navigates it reveals judgement at altitude.
  • AI fluency (Level 2 and above). How the candidate works with AI, what they cross-check, and where they draw the line.
  • Data interpretation (Level 3). A commercial dashboard. The candidate surfaces the three things the principal actually needs to know, ordered by weight.
  • Mindset check. A short set of calibrated questions that surface approach and orientation. How the candidate thinks about the seat itself, not just the tasks inside it.

There is no multiple-choice shortcut. Every response is written in the candidate's own words, which is where judgement becomes visible.

The clock matters

Every session is timed. When the clock hits zero the session auto-submits. Candidates cannot pause or extend. This mirrors the reality that a working day ends whether you're ready or not. How a candidate handles the pressure is part of what's being measured.

Session durations by level

  • Level 1, Operational EA: 60 minutes
  • Level 2, EA to Senior Leadership: 75 minutes
  • Level 3, Executive Business Partner: 90 minutes
  • Private PA, Level 1: 70 minutes

Answers save as you go. Candidates can refresh the page if something goes wrong. Accommodations for neurodivergence and disability add 25% to the session length on self-certified request.

Why It Works

Interviews, psychometrics, CVs. They each miss something.

A CV tells you where someone has been. It cannot tell you whether they'd survive your Tuesday morning. An interview tests confidence and preparation. It rewards candidates who rehearse well, not candidates who think well. A psychometric reports what a candidate says about themselves. It does not capture what they do.

The Core Standard puts the candidate inside a realistic day and watches how they move. That is a different kind of evidence. The kind you only get on their second week in the seat. Except you get it before the offer goes out.
